# Early Adulthood

I started looking for a job as I waited for the university application to kick off. Luckily, I got lucky and landed, on 2nd March, a home job at a retired high school principal's home — I was a house boy. I cleaned the home, did the normal chores, and took care of the pets and livestock, where I got a monthly pay of 5,000 Ksh. I spent most of the money buying data bundles for my research and entertainment.

Since I had no other option left except tech, I started researching the fields in tech and started learning programming, being guided by random YouTubers. I applied for a Bachelor of Science in Software Engineering at Kisii University and got admitted on 21st August 2025. I was already ahead of my group, but still kept attending the lectures.

In the first semester, I created a group where I could teach my fellow coursemates coding — we called it **The Elites Coding Group**. I later developed a CLI app that could help my female friends manage their periods/menstruation. I showed my friends (in the coding group and other close friends), and they liked it, but it was too simple. As comrades, they needed something else. That's where my innovation journey officially started. I now had no restrictions. I added features that buddies wanted (they wanted the app to show the days when the lady was too fertile, to avoid unprotected sex — shown in red; yellow were moderate days when the lady was less fertile; and green when the lady was very infertile, hence could just engage in unprotected sex without worrying about getting pregnant). I shipped it, and they liked it this time.

Something told me it could become something greater, and hence I started building it from scratch into a real app. I decided to explore the whole medical field and identified some gaps in the healthcare system. I took my friend James, from the group, to help me develop it. Anyways, I was studying the world's richest people and discovered that most were in tech, and decided to create a startup. The app was the flagship, though it was never registered as a startup — we believed it to be one. James and I got a team of 5 to work on the app. We called it **Novela** — a unified healthcare app. I made James lead its development.

The team members gave up due to classwork and the technicality of the ideas I was presenting to them; some wanted payment, which obviously I couldn't afford. That left me and James to work on it on our own.

In the meantime, I started another startup called **Sorabbyngo** — a name I got since high school, without knowing its meaning. It was originally intended to focus on AI development, but since I never had people who could help me build the models or fine-tune the existing models, I started to work on a different venture: **Sorabbyngo OS**. It was inspired by sci-fi movies like *Her*, *Transcendence*, *Upgrade*, *Ex Machina*, JARVIS from *Iron Man*, *Chappie*, among others — I really liked sci-fi movies.

I started working on a new type of software combining OS and AI — an "agentic OS," and called it Sorabbyngo OS. It looked very ridiculously ambitious that no one was ready to help me develop it. Meanwhile, James was still working on the Novela app. Since I never had the pro skills to develop the entire OS, I studied the existing Linux distros deeply, and forked the Linux kernel, customised it, and started adding the AI runtime layers into it. The OS was meant to be self-improving, but that raised some issues of trust — what if the AI goes rogue and rewrites the kernel codes. Due to lack of funding to upgrade my PC, I decided to stop the development of the OS. I embarked on Novela's backend system as James did the frontend. By the end of 1.2 (second semester of first year), we had already made a prototype.

I went to visit my uncle, Julius, for 2 months during the long April holiday — who happened to be in the Kenya Air Force. Still with my passion in military activities and engineering, I started working on cyber-defence projects, which I combined with UAVs, and made another startup called **T13 Systems**. Anyways, my uncle helped financially in my projects, which I really appreciated. The weight of the projects — I could barely keep up with the academic activities at school. I decided to drop out of the university and focus on building them up, though I stayed near the school to keep up with my friends and the free resources the school had. Now I was 20 years old when I dropped out — I never officially attended any class from there.

That's the present day as per today. I will keep on adding to it as I grow.
