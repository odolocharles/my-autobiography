# School Years

We lived most of our lives along River Yala, and hence spent most of my schooling around Bondo. My dad never had a steady job that could raise us and educate us well. There was nobody to intervene and help, thus leaving my mom with the job. There was no economic activity around there that could maybe raise us, except weeding and ploughing for rich neighbours, and using the small wages wisely.

Family stress and poverty, alongside other family issues, drove my dad into excessive alcohol consumption. He could become very lazy, unproductive, and irresponsible. My mom took all the family responsibilities (even the burden of feeding and raising him too). Despite all that, he could always tell/encourage us to work smart — from the conditions we were at, and being that we're brilliant kids, he always believed that we could someday make it. That earned him a name, *Ti Mariek* ("work smart" in Luo dialect), around the neighbourhoods and workplaces.

Meanwhile, my humble mother could go and work in the farms, despite rejections and abuse from the village women, as they said she's a teacher hence couldn't do those dirty jobs. That really humbled us — especially me, being the eldest and the only son.

I finished my primary education in March 2021, despite the challenges (including Coronavirus and floods, major pandemics that shook the whole world, and that led to disruption of the education calendar, making us do our final KCPE (Kenya Certificate of Primary Education) exams in March the following year, and meaning that we were to join our high schools in September the same year), and managed to get a B+ (372/500).

The holiday after my KCPE was very horrible. My parents parted ways and split, which somehow affected my KCPE since the fight started earlier, before exams commenced. After some legal proceedings, we decided to follow our mom — we couldn't live with dad, as he was always depressed, angry, drunk, and also violent, hence unstable; we didn't see our futures there. Luckily, mom was officially employed by the TSC (Teachers' Service Commission), meaning she could now take loans and finance our education, since she was now employed by the government.
